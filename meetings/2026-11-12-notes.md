TSC Recruitment
---------------

Lydia: Doesn't feel it would be bad to invite people to join given the
state of funding and such.  Volunteering for an organization in thie
capacity doesn't feel like false pretenses.  We're open to changes,
pretty up-front about our project's status.  Failing to take steps to
encourage people to join would be self-defeating.

Engin: It wouldn't hurt to ask around and let people refuse if they
find it weak or unappealing.  Midway solution could be to not do it
until we meet again next time to see what the situation is after a few
months without funding.  E.g., based on whether we are actually doing
something for the project, it might feel more encouraging to pursue
(or not).

Brad: Could brainstorm about who to invite in meantime since that will
take time in any case.

Daniel: Two orthogonal-ish aspects w.r.t. Chapel surviving.  1) it
needs a TSC; 2) it needs to not bit-rot as a technology.  Reason to
consider hesitating to invite people is the possibility of sustaining
the organization without sustaining the technology.  Might suggest
taking a wait-and-see attitude.  Project doesn't need to grow or add
new features, but if it bit-rots then the TSC is less useful/valuable.
Daniel would wait and see for a little bit.  It's not like we can't
survive without new TSC members.  Doing recruiting now feels
premature.

Andy: Would help to have some sort of vision statement.  "This is what
we'd like to see over next six months to year" since people will want
to know where it's going.  Do we intend to have ChapelCon-like events?
Other infrastructure to develop not tied to HPE resources?

Brad: Any disagreements with brainstorming about who to invite and
taking this up next meeting?

Lydia: Not sure...  There's a point where hesitancy can have negative
impact as well.  If we're not willing to say we'd do something in that
time, maybe that speaks to winding down the project.

Daniel: Some might say that, previously, our desire to hold off on
community growth until we had a more polished language arguably harmed
us s.t. we lost momentum.  There could be a symmetric argument here.
Do we do ourselves a dis-service by not starting on this now?
(realizing this is in opposition to previous statement).

Ben: Whether we want to grow TSC and what we want to do over next 6-12
months feel distinct.  Growing the TSC is more justifiable if we have
a concrete plan.

Daniel: Strongly agree.

Brad: Advertise on social media, website, etc.?

Lydia: Hesitant about this due to worry about unrelated people (or
bots?) wandering in and causing noise.

Daniel: Thinks we're not so popular that random people wuld care
enough to apply.  Sees no reason to prevent people who are that
enthusiastic from talking to us.  If it becomes a problem, we can
pause and reconsider.

Jade: Similar to Daniel, thinks if we suddenly had random people
wanting to join the TSC, that's a good problem to have and we should
deal with it then.  Doesn't think we should be hiding this due to fear
of noise in the future — closes ourselves off to it prematurely.
Agrees with Daniel's earlier point about not growing by hiding away.

Brad: Remember that the form doesn't leave the doors wide open, but
has screens and expectations.  If a newbie showed up without
sufficient experience, could recruit them to help with bitrot issue
Daniel brought up as a way of building their reputation with the
project.

Daniel: TSC and the language itself are fairly separate.  Are we that
crucial as a governing body, and is the body meant to be that much
bigger than it is now?

Brad: More diversity in membership (i.e., not just former HPE
employees) could send positive signals to potential sources of
funding.

Lydia: W.r.t. TSC members needing to prove investment and interest, if
we're not sure whether we're going to ramp up or down, do we want a
bunch of new, potential people that we've never heard of come in, do
vibe-coded PRs, maybe without programming experience, etc.  That puts
a potential burden on us in terms of what we're signing ourselves up
for.  Would we be overwhelmed by that?  The bar for them to clear
becomes work for us to decide what we want to do, which may in turn
become a forcing function for what happens to the organization going
forward.  If half of PRs are things people won't have the bandwidth to
review, are we setting ourselves up for failure?  Even though not many
humans are looking for such things, bots could make things spammy.

Jade: Thinks we're shooting ourselves in the foot if we worry about
problems that don't exist.  If we're overwhelmed by bots, can worry
about it then.  It's not our job anymore to respond to bots in a
timely fashion.  If we're overwhelmed, we don't have to respond.
We're individuals and it's not our day job anymore.  Doesn't hope we
get overwhelmed and spammed, but even with past year+ of AI bots, we
really haven't been overhwelmed.  Wants to avoid us cowering from the
fight before it happens.

Daniel: Agrees.  "What if too many contributors?" feels like too
hypothetical a problem, esp. since we've always welcomed PRs and
contributions.  We have seen some misguided PRs, but haven't seen
malicious/self-serving actions.  If you're submitting a slop PR,
you're giving us permission to ignore you.  If volume is too difficult
for us to process b/c we can't assess quality or go through PRs to
detect LLMs or bots, that feels like several orders of magnitude than
any contributions we've seen so far.  Doesn't feel we should base our
decision-making on that possibility.

Lydia: Thinks that's fair.  Worries about seeing that sort of
overwhelm based on data from GSoC Mentors list.  People have had to
close independent contributions b/c have an overwhelming amount of it.
And we don't have the Google clout.


External-to-HPE Committers
--------------------------

Jade: Speaking as a soon-to-be-non-HPE member, feels strongly that we
need to have some way to give merge privileges to people outside of
HPE because there will be few people within HPE that can merge code,
such that it'll become a bottleneck, and dampen the enthusiasm of
external contributors.  Will also give us a nice bump in terms of HPSF
status.  As far as making HPE comfortable, all Jenkins configs get
pretty substantial smoke testing within GA already.  Not sure whether
that's enough or not.  Not eager to rip down HPE testing, but if
that's the only way to permit external mergers, thinks we should.

Daniel: Thinks there's a distinction between merge privileges and
trusted reviews.  Hard to imagine the latter being a concern.  Two
external contributors could develop and review code.  Someone within
HPE could push the button if external people are still trusted enough
to merge code.

Engin: Imagines winding down HPE testing going forward makes sense
given funding situation.  If we make further releases, should just be
up-front about what testing isn't covered.  Add "testing Chapel" as
being another way to contribute to Chapel.  E.g., community member
with access to HPE or GPU resources.

Andy: It would be a shame not to take advantage of testing resources
as long as we have them.  Could we have an HPE mirror that someone
would occasionally sync to the main/public repository, serving as a
gatekeeper.

Brad: To Daniel and Andy's points, while the approaches are
attractive, I think that ultimately the person/people inside would
become rubber stamps.  Which is to say, if I trust Daniel and Jade to
develop and review a PR externally without reviewing the code myself,
I'm likely to just merge it without thought, in which case I may as
well give them merge privileges.  Similarly, it seems unlikely I'd do
a good job of reviewing code before merging it to the mirror if I
wasn't the original reviewer.

Lydia: [redacted] could be a good person to check with on HPE
policies.
